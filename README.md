# How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating “user stories” help with this?
When using Agile, understanding what users need is really about putting yourself in their shoes and figuring out what they’re actually trying to do, not just what they say. You break their needs into smaller pieces so it’s easier to build. That’s where user stories help a lot. They keep things simple by saying something like “As a user, I want ___ so that ___,” which makes it clear what you’re building and why. It helps keep the focus on the user instead of just coding random features.

# How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?
For developing programs, Agile is more about taking things step by step instead of trying to finish everything all at once. I’d start small, test as I go, and make changes based on feedback. It’s a lot more flexible that way. I’d want to use things like sprints, stand-ups, and reviews because they help keep everyone on track and make sure problems get caught early instead of at the end

# What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?
Being a good team member in software development is mostly about communication and working well with others. You can’t just do your own thing and ignore the team. You’ve got to share ideas, be open to feedback, and help out when needed. It also means being reliable and doing your part so the team can move forward together.
